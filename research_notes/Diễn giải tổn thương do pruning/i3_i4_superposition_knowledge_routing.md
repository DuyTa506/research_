## I3: Academic work on superposition/polysemanticity and pruning/compression; SAE before-vs-after comparisons

### Takeaway
A small but fast-growing 2026 literature now trains SAEs on a dense LLM and tracks which features survive weight pruning or quantization. The consistent finding is that perplexity hides feature damage, and that **high-firing-rate (frequent, generic) features are destroyed first while rare, specialized features survive**. Survival in geometric terms does not predict causal importance. No paper found here uses SAEs or transcoders to compare **structured** pruning (neurons vs layers) or to analyse pruning followed by distillation. Older work (Gurnee 2023, Scherlis 2022, Voita 2023) gives the supporting premises: early-layer MLP neurons are heavily polysemantic, polysemanticity is the optimal way to spend limited capacity, and many neurons are dead and can be pruned for free.

### Cited Findings

**SAE analyses of compressed LLMs (2026; all NV)**
- **Borobia, Seguí-Mas, Tormo-Carbó, "How Pruning Reshapes Features: SAE Analysis of Weight-Pruned LMs", arXiv 2603.25325 (2026), NV.** TopK SAEs on residual-stream activations with 3–5 seeds per condition; unstructured magnitude vs Wanda pruning. Rare features survive better than frequent ones. arXiv v1 HTML: "ρ≤-0.8 between firing rate and survival in 15/17 conditions". The abstract version says "within-condition Spearman correlations of rho = -1.0 in 11 of 17 experimental conditions". **The two figures conflict, so check the PDF.** The authors read this as pruning acting as "implicit feature selection preferentially destroying high-frequency generic features while preserving specialized rare ones". Wanda "preserves feature structure up to 3.7x better than magnitude pruning" **[secondary summary]**. Geometric feature survival "does not predict causal importance". Practical recommendation: train an SAE on the dense model, and features in the high-firing-rate quintiles (Q4–Q5) are the most at risk. — [arXiv 2603.25325](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25325); [awesomepapers summary](https://awesomepapers.io/llm-papers/papers/2603.25325)
- **Journal version / extension, "Feature survival under weight pruning: SAE diagnostics for compressed language models", Neurocomputing (ScienceDirect, 2026), NV.** Models: Gemma 3 1B, Gemma 2 2B, Llama 3.2 1B. Extends to Wanda 2:4 and int8/int4 quantization. "Low-firing-rate SAE features show higher geometric survival under pruning than high-firing-rate features"; "geometric feature survival does not predict local ablation impact". Wanda preserves SAE feature geometry better than magnitude pruning at matched sparsity, and a fixed-dictionary control gives the same ordering. Firing rate is described as "a simple pre-pruning indicator of feature fragility". — [ScienceDirect S0925231226022241](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0925231226022241)
- **"How Quantization Changes Interpretable Features: An SAE Analysis of LMs" / "Perplexity Can Miss SAE Feature Damage Under Quantization", arXiv 2606.03002 (2026), NV.** Perplexity can stay flat or even improve while many SAE features degrade. Quantization damages many of the same features as pruning at matched perplexity. Feature change under quantization is graded, with no sharp precision cliff. — [arXiv 2606.03002](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03002)
- **Khalili et al., "When Pruning Meets Interpretability: Preserving SAE Robustness in LLMs", arXiv 2608.25941, COLM 2026, NV.** Middle layers are "significantly more sensitive to pruning than early or late layers" in terms of SAE robustness. The paper proposes a layer-wise sparsity allocation based on this. Code: github.com/KhaliliMahdi/sae-robustness-under-pruning. — [arXiv 2608.25941](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25941); [GitHub](https://github.com/KhaliliMahdi/sae-robustness-under-pruning)

**SAE transfer across related checkpoints (fine-tuning used as a proxy for "after modification")**
- **Kissane, robertzk, Conmy, Nanda, "SAEs (usually) Transfer Between Base and Chat Models", AI Alignment Forum, 2024, NV (blog post).** Base-model SAEs reconstruct chat-model activations "surprisingly well" (and vice versa) on Mistral-7B and Qwen1.5-0.5B. Splicing in the base SAE gives CE loss similar to the chat SAE. The SAEs fail on outlier-norm tokens such as BOS: these are under 1% of activations but cause cascading errors. Transfer fails on Gemma v1 2B, which the authors attribute to an unusually large base–chat weight difference. Fine-tuning the base SAE on 5M chat activations becomes competitive with a chat SAE trained from scratch. — [Alignment Forum](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/fmwk6qxrpW8d4jvbd/saes-usually-transfer-between-base-and-chat-models)
- Follow-up evidence: a refusal-direction study on Gemma found that chat SAEs reconstruct the refusal direction at cosine 0.98 vs 0.86 for a base SAE **[secondary, via search summary]**. — [arXiv 2505.23556](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23556)
- **Lan, Torr, Barez et al., "Sparse Autoencoders Reveal Universal Feature Spaces Across LLMs" (later retitled "Quantifying Feature Space Universality Across LLMs via SAEs"), arXiv 2410.06981, NV.** Match SAE features across models by activation correlation, then apply SVCCA and RSA. Reports "significant similarities in SAE feature spaces across various LLMs". Semantic subspaces such as emotions and time are especially similar. Caveat: different SAEs do not consistently learn the same universal features. Exact SVCCA values were not retrieved. — [arXiv 2410.06981](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.06981)

**Older premises: superposition, polysemanticity, dead neurons**
- **Gurnee et al., "Finding Neurons in a Haystack: Case Studies with Sparse Probing", arXiv 2305.01610, TMLR 2023, NV.** k-sparse probes over 100+ features in 10 categories, 7 models from 70M to 6.9B. "Early layers make use of sparse combinations of neurons to represent many features in superposition". Middle layers have more dedicated neurons for higher-level contextual features (e.g., "is Python code"). Weight statistics plus toy-model reasoning suggest the first ~quarter of MLP layers carry substantially more superposition. — [arXiv 2305.01610](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.01610); [ar5iv mirror](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2305.01610)
- **Scherlis, Sachan, Jermyn, Benton, Shlegeris, "Polysemanticity and Capacity in Neural Networks", arXiv 2210.01892 (2022), NV.** Defines feature capacity as the fraction of an embedding dimension a feature uses, with the total bounded by the dimension. Under optimal allocation, the model represents the most important features monosemantically, represents less important features polysemantically in proportion to their effect on loss, and ignores the least important ones. Polysemanticity rises with input kurtosis/sparsity and varies by architecture. The embedding space becomes block-semi-orthogonal. — [arXiv 2210.01892](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.01892)
- **Voita, Ferrando, Nalmpantis, "Neurons in LLMs: Dead, N-gram, Positional", arXiv 2309.04827, Findings of ACL 2024 (pp. 1288–1301), NV.** In OPT models, "more than 70% in some layers of the 66b model" of FFN neurons are dead, meaning they never activate on a large, diverse dataset. Many live early-layer neurons act as token/n-gram detectors. OPT-350M is an outlier with no dead neurons. Per-model percentages are in Fig. 1 but were not retrieved. — [arXiv 2309.04827](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.04827); [Meta AI page](https://ai.meta.com/research/publications/neurons-in-large-language-models-dead-n-gram-positional)
- **Cunningham et al., SAEs find highly interpretable features, arXiv 2309.08600, NV.** Listed for completeness. Its content was not retrieved in this session, so no claims are made about it here. — [arXiv 2309.08600](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.08600)

**Concentration of loss sensitivity in FFN channels (structured pruning)**
- **"Supernodes and Halos: Loss-Critical Hubs in LLM Feed-Forward Layers", arXiv 2604.23475 (Apr 2026), NV.** Uses a Fisher-style loss proxy (LP) from activation-gradient second moments. In Llama-3.1-8B, the top 1% of FFN channels per layer hold a median of **58.7%** of LP mass (range 33.0–86.1%). These "supernodes" overlap only weakly with activation outliers and are not explained by activation power or weight norm. "Halos" are channels that share or read the residual coordinates a supernode writes into. One-shot structured FFN pruning at 50% sparsity: the SCAR-Prot variant reaches **PPL 54.8 vs 989.2 for Wanda-channel**, because baselines that prune supernodes degrade sharply. — [arXiv 2604.23475](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.23475); [emergentmind](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2604.23475)

**Pruning in toy superposition models**
- No paper found that prunes neurons in a toy superposition model and measures recoverability. The search returned only the Toy Models paper itself (Anthropic, out of scope) and reviews. — [search result: AF post "Superposition is not just neuron polysemanticity"](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/8EyCQKuWo6swZpagS/superposition-is-not-just-neuron-polysemanticity)

### Inferences
- In SAE work on weight pruning, damage concentrates on **high-frequency, generic** features. For an OCR VLM, those are the features used on almost every token: layout, script, and common-glyph features. This differs from the classic "pruning hurts the rare long tail" story (see I4). The two results are not contradictory: one measures feature *geometry*, the other measures *task outputs*. They do mean that "what is lost" depends on the measurement.
- Gurnee (early-layer superposition) and Scherlis (capacity is used polysemantically when it is scarce) together suggest that removing an MLP neuron deletes slices of *many* features, each a nonlinear (gated) detector. An optimal linear map at the cut cannot synthesise a missing nonlinear detector, so this damage should show up mainly as **D_res**. That is consistent with the user's finding that neuron cuts recover worse per unit damage. **This is inference; no source tests it.**
- Supernodes/halos (58.7% of FFN loss sensitivity in 1% of channels) imply that neuron-pruning damage is extremely heavy-tailed. A few mis-scored channels can dominate zero-shot damage, and if those channels are nonlinear hubs, D_res is large. This suggests checking whether the user's neuron-cut damage is dominated by a few channels.
- Voita's dead neurons imply that part of any neuron-pruning budget is free, with near-zero damage. If the user's per-damage comparison excludes them, the ~10x gap may be partly an artefact of how the neuron budget is spent.

### Gaps
- No found work uses SAEs/transcoders/crosscoders to compare features **before vs after structured (neuron/head/layer) pruning, or after pruning + distillation**. All 2026 SAE-compression papers study unstructured/semi-structured weight pruning (magnitude, Wanda, 2:4) and quantization. "What is lost in distillation via SAE" returned nothing relevant.
- No study measures polysemanticity (e.g., neuron SAE-feature count or sparse-probe k) against per-neuron prunability or recoverability.
- No pruning experiments in toy superposition models were found outside Anthropic's own work.
- None of this applies to hybrid linear-attention (GatedDeltaNet) models or to VLM/OCR models. All evidence is from text-only standard transformers.
- Exact SVCCA values (2410.06981) and the correct correlation figure for 2603.25325 could not be verified because arXiv is blocked.

## I4: Knowledge vs routing roles of unit types; what each unit type's removal loses

### Takeaway
The academic picture is fairly consistent. **MLP/FFN units store parametric knowledge** (key-value memories; mid/early-layer MLPs at the last subject token are causal for facts). **Attention mostly routes and copies**, and is largely redundant except for a sparse set of retrieval/induction heads (<5% of heads). Matching this, dropping attention sublayers is nearly free (half of Llama-2-70B's attention layers removed costs 2.4%), while dropping MLPs hurts. Compression of any kind hurts **fact recall** first (degradation from more than 30% size reduction) while **in-context processing** survives 60–70% reduction. No paper was found that mechanistically explains *why neuron (width) pruning is less recoverable than layer (depth) pruning*. The literature in fact partly points the other way at fixed parameter count after heavy retraining (Minitron: width better than depth). Only partial mechanisms exist: residual iterative refinement and middle-layer redundancy explain why layers are removable.

### Cited Findings

**MLP = knowledge store**
- **Geva, Schuster, Berant, Levy, "Transformer Feed-Forward Layers Are Key-Value Memories", EMNLP 2021 (arXiv 2012.14913), NV.** FFN keys correlate with textual patterns. "Lower layers tend to capture shallow patterns, while upper layers learn more semantic ones". Values induce a next-token distribution, which matters most in upper layers. — [arXiv 2012.14913](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.14913); [ACL Anthology preview](https://preview.aclanthology.org/setup/2021.emnlp-main.446)
- **Geva et al. 2022, arXiv 2203.14680 (FFN layers promote concepts in vocabulary space):** not retrieved in this session. No claims made.
- **Meng, Bau, Andonian, Belinkov, ROME, "Locating and Editing Factual Associations in GPT", arXiv 2202.05262, NeurIPS 2022, NV.** Causal tracing (corrupt the subject, then restore individual clean states) shows that "mid-layer MLP modules at the last token of the subject" have a strong causal effect on factual predictions. Rank-one edits are most successful at the last subject token in middle layers. In their comparison, Knowledge Neurons editing "seems unable to make effective edits". — [arXiv 2202.05262](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.05262)
- **Dai et al., "Knowledge Neurons in Pretrained Transformers", ACL 2022, NV.** Gradient-based attribution selects FFN neurons for a fact. The suppression/amplification percentages could not be verified, because aclanthology.org is unreachable and no search excerpt gave them. — (gap; see Gaps)
- **Geva, Bastings, Filippova, Globerson, "Dissecting Recall of Factual Associations in Auto-Regressive LMs", arXiv 2304.14767, EMNLP 2023, NV.** Three steps: (1) **early MLP sublayers** enrich the subject representation. The paper stresses lower MLPs over the mid-layer MLPs highlighted by ROME. (2) Relation information propagates to the last token. (3) **Attention heads extract** the attribute, and heads "often hold subject-attribute mappings in their parameters", in ~70% of predictions. In GPT-2, the MLP extraction rate is 31.3%, well below MHSA. Blocking attention to subject positions sharply suppresses extraction. — [arXiv 2304.14767](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.14767); [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2304.14767). [secondary] A third-party summary reports that MLP knockouts cut attribute recall by ~88% vs <30% for MHSA knockouts. Not verified. — [sidn.baulab.info](https://sidn.baulab.info/association/)

**Attention = routing/copying; a sparse set of heads is critical**
- **Wu, Wang, Xiao, Peng, Fu, "Retrieval Head Mechanistically Explains Long-Context Factuality", arXiv 2404.15574, ICLR 2025, NV.** Retrieval heads are identified by a copy-paste retrieval score on Needle-in-a-Haystack (follow-ups use RS ≥ 0.01). They are **<5% of heads** and universal across long-context models. Masking them causes retrieval failure and hallucination, while masking random non-retrieval heads does not. Output stays fluent but wrong, so the heads matter for factual retrieval rather than general language ability. [secondary numbers] LLaMA-2-7B-80K: masking the top-20 retrieval heads drops NIAH accuracy to **63.6%**, vs **94.7%** when masking 20 random heads. A downstream QA F1 drops **56.7 → 47.5** when 50 retrieval heads are masked. — [arXiv 2404.15574](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.15574); [ICLR 2025](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2025/poster/30373); [Liner review](https://liner.com/review/retrieval-head-mechanistically-explains-longcontext-factuality)
- **Crosbie & Shutova, "Induction Heads as an Essential Mechanism for Pattern Matching in ICL", arXiv 2407.07011, Findings of NAACL 2025, NV.** Ablating the top 1–3% of induction heads in Llama-3-8B / InternLM2-20B causes ICL drops of up to **~32%** on abstract pattern-recognition tasks, far more than random-head ablation. Blocking only the induction attention pattern gives drops comparable to full head ablation. — [arXiv 2407.07011](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.07011)
- arXiv 2404.07129 (induction-head formation): ablating a single head is "almost unobservable" except for the strongest head, which points to redundancy among heads. — [arXiv 2404.07129](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07129)

**Sublayer-type drop experiments (attention vs MLP vs block)**
- **He et al., "What Matters in Transformers? Not All Attention is Needed", arXiv 2406.15786, NV.** Llama-2-70B: pruning half the attention layers gives a **48.4% speedup with a 2.4% performance drop**. "Dropping MLP layers hurts performance". Attention redundancy is consistent across training checkpoints. Dropping 31 layers (attention + MLP) still retains 90% of Llama-2-13B's MMLU performance. [secondary] Speedup-degradation ratio for dropping 8 modules in Llama-2-13B: **Attention 0.02 vs Block 0.31 vs MLP 0.79**. KV cache shrinks 52→26 GB. — [arXiv 2406.15786](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.15786); [HF page](https://huggingface.co/papers/2406.15786)
- **Zhang et al., "FinerCut: Finer-grained Interpretable Layer Pruning for LLMs", arXiv 2405.18218, NeurIPS 2024 workshop, NV.** Attention and FFN sublayers are scored as separate pruning candidates. On Llama3-70B, **34 of 80 self-attention layers (~42%)** can be removed while keeping ~99% performance, without fine-tuning. Retains 90% of Llama3-8B at 25% layer removal and 95% of Llama3-70B at 30%. Deeper self-attention layers are "more redundant than FFNs", and the selected removals are mostly attention layers in consecutive blocks. — [arXiv 2405.18218](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.18218); [NeurIPS virtual](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2024/98196)

**Which capabilities are lost under compression**
- **Jin, Clement, Dong, …, Carbin, Dziugaite, "The Cost of Down-Scaling LMs: Fact Recall Deteriorates before In-Context Learning", arXiv 2310.04680, ICLR 2024, NV.** Covers both weight pruning and dense scaling. Removing more than 30% of weights gives significant (>5% relative) degradation on fact-recall tasks. Pruning 60% of weights gives <5% relative degradation on ICL tasks. Dense down-scaling shows the same asymmetry. — [arXiv 2310.04680](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.04680); [mlanthology](https://mlanthology.org/iclr/2024/jin2024iclr-cost/)
- **Jaiswal et al., LLM-KICK, "Compressing LLMs: The Truth is Rarely Pure and Never Simple", arXiv 2310.01382, ICLR 2024, NV.** Knowledge-intensive factoid QA degrades substantially at only ~25–30% sparsity while perplexity barely moves. All SoTA pruning methods fail for N:M structured sparsity. 8-bit quantization costs ~8–10%, much less than pruning. Pruned models at ≥50% sparsity remain "robust retrieval systems" and summarisers in in-context settings, but longer context suffers more. — [arXiv 2310.01382](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01382); [ICLR proceedings](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/9f09f316a3eaf59d9ced5ffaefe97e0f-Abstract-Conference.html); [GitHub](https://github.com/VITA-Group/llm-kick)
- **Gromov et al., "The Unreasonable Ineffectiveness of the Deeper Layers", arXiv 2403.17887, ICLR 2025, NV.** QA benchmarks tolerate removing deep layers well, while math/commonsense reasoning "exhibited immediate sensitivity to any layer removal" **[secondary characterisation]**. — [ICLR 2025 PDF](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/cbabc2f70de2dd09f491a8715ec3e80f-Paper-Conference.pdf)
- **"Demystifying the Roles of LLM Layers in Retrieval, Knowledge, and Reasoning", arXiv 2510.02091 (2025), NV.** LLaMA-3.1-8B: ablating the first few layers causes sharp drops on retrieval tasks (up to −0.8 accuracy), and beyond the lower layers retrieval stays near-perfect. Likelihood metrics make deep layers look dispensable, but generation-based evaluation shows middle/deep layers are needed for reasoning and long-range coherence. Reasoning often depends on a few specialised heads. [secondary] Distillation can spread reasoning capacity across more layers, which makes the model more robust to pruning. — [arXiv 2510.02091](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02091); [GitHub](https://github.com/Hik289/llm-layer-importance)
- **Hooker, Courville, Clark, Dauphin, Frome, "What Do Compressed Deep Neural Networks Forget?", arXiv 1911.05248 (v3 2021), NV (vision, outside the 2019+ text focus but foundational).** "Pruning Identified Exemplars" (PIEs) are a small subset systematically more affected by sparsity. Compression "disproportionately impacts model performance on the underrepresented long-tail". Models with very different weight counts match on top-line metrics but diverge on a narrow slice. — [Google Research](https://research.google/pubs/what-do-compressed-deep-neural-networks-forget/)
- **"The Rediscovery Hypothesis", arXiv 2103.01819, NV.** Tokens rare in the probing corpus lose more accuracy as weight pruning increases, which suggests memorised information is removed at higher pruning rates. — [arXiv 2103.01819](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.01819)
- Contrast from SAE work (I3): weight pruning destroys *high-frequency* SAE features first (2603.25325). "Rare tokens/examples hurt at the output" and "frequent features hurt in representation geometry" are both reported. — [arXiv 2603.25325](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25325)

**Layer removability and depth vs width (towards a mechanism)**
- **Lad, Lee, Gurnee, Tegmark, "The Remarkable Robustness of LLMs: Stages of Inference?", arXiv 2406.19384, NeurIPS 2025, NV.** Deleting or swapping adjacent layers without fine-tuning keeps **72–95%** of original top-1 accuracy (GPT-2 and Pythia, 8 models). Early and final layers are the most fragile, and middle layers are very robust. Proposed stages: detokenization → feature engineering → prediction ensembling → residual sharpening/calibration. — [arXiv 2406.19384](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.19384); [NeurIPS 2025](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/poster/117558)
- **Jastrzębski et al., "Residual Connections Encourage Iterative Inference", arXiv 1710.04773, ICLR 2018, NV (pre-2019, background).** Residual blocks iteratively refine a representation in a shared feature space, so individual blocks can be dropped. — [arXiv 1710.04773](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.04773)
- **"Residual Stream Analysis with Multi-Layer SAEs", arXiv 2409.04185, NV.** Adjacent-layer residual activations are "fairly similar", and many latents are active at multiple layers. Caveat: for individual prompts, most latents are active at a single layer. — [arXiv 2409.04185](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.04185)
- **Kim et al., "Shortened LLaMA: Depth Pruning for LLMs", arXiv 2402.02834, NV.** With LoRA retraining at moderate ratios, depth pruning rivals width pruning on zero-shot tasks. Beyond 40% removal, full-parameter continued pretraining is crucial and markedly beats LoRA. — [arXiv 2402.02834](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.02834)
- **Sreenivas et al., "LLM Pruning and Distillation in Practice: The Minitron Approach", arXiv 2408.11796, NV.** At equal parameter count after distillation, **width pruning outperforms depth**. Llama-3.1-Minitron-4B GSM8K: width 41.2% vs depth 16.8%. — [arXiv 2408.11796](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.11796)
- **arXiv 2609.19579, "Recovering Aggressively Pruned VLA Models with Offline Hidden-State Distillation" (2026), NV.** Their explanation of depth vs width: "a removed layer eliminates every computation it performed, while a narrowed layer retains the highest-scoring heads and channels at every depth". Depth-pruned OpenVLA-OFT reaches 0% success at all three points, while 45% width pruning keeps 54.4%. This is a **starting-point** explanation in favour of width, the opposite direction to the user's finding. — [arXiv 2609.19579](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19579)

### Inferences
- **Mapping to an OCR VLM.** MLP neurons (especially early/mid decoder MLPs) are the likely store of "vocabulary knowledge": glyph→token, rare words, language-specific tokens. Attention, and possibly the GatedDeltaNet mixers, mainly do routing and copying: reading-order tracking and copying visual tokens into text. The prediction is that neuron cuts lose rare tokens and lexicon knowledge, while layer cuts (whole blocks, especially attention-heavy, middle ones) lose order, format, and long-range copying, *unless* a retrieval/induction-like head sits in a cut layer. This is a testable hypothesis, not a finding.
- **Towards the ~10x recoverability gap (synthesis, not a quoted result).** (a) Under iterative refinement, a middle block's contribution is a small update in a shared basis, and adjacent layers are similar (Lad 2024; 2409.04185). Its removal is therefore partly compensable by a linear map at the cut and by self-repair in neighbouring layers, giving high D_lin and low D_res. (b) An MLP neuron is a nonlinear key that detects a pattern (Geva 2021). In superposition (Gurnee 2023; Scherlis 2022) it carries slices of many features, and loss sensitivity sits in a few hub channels (Supernodes, 58.7% in 1%). Deleting it removes stored associations that no linear map can recreate, and distillation must re-learn them from data. Fact recall is also the capability compression hurts first (Jin 2024; LLM-KICK). Together this predicts the user's pattern: per unit of zero-shot damage, neuron damage is mostly D_res (lost knowledge), and layer damage is mostly D_lin/routing (re-derivable).
- **Reconciling with Minitron.** Minitron/2609.19579 compare at *equal parameter count* after retraining, where width wins because depth's zero-shot damage is much larger. The user normalises *per unit of zero-shot damage*, which is a different axis. Both can hold: depth damage is large but "cheap" (recoverable), and width damage is smaller but "expensive" (knowledge). This fits LLM-KICK and Jin, where perplexity hides knowledge loss.
- Retrieval-head sparsity (<5%) suggests a risk for layer cutting. If a cut layer contains a copy/retrieval head, OCR-style copying could fail catastrophically while perplexity stays fine. Retrieval-score screening before choosing which layers to cut is a cheap check.

### Gaps
- **No paper found that mechanistically explains why neuron/width pruning is less recoverable than layer/depth pruning (per unit damage).** The only explicit depth-vs-width explanation found (2609.19579) argues the opposite direction, in favour of width, at equal parameters. Shortened LLaMA and Minitron disagree on depth vs width depending on the retraining regime. The synthesis above is inference.
- Dai et al. 2022 (knowledge neurons) suppression/amplification percentages and Geva 2022 (2203.14680) could not be retrieved because ACL Anthology is unreachable and arXiv is blocked.
- No study directly measures **token-frequency vs damage** for structured pruning of LLMs. Evidence is from vision (Hooker) and unstructured pruning (Rediscovery Hypothesis, LLM-KICK, Jin). "Memorization in pruned models" searches returned nothing on point.
- No evidence on knowledge vs routing roles in **GatedDeltaNet / linear-attention hybrids or in OCR/VLM decoders**. All results are from standard softmax-attention text LLMs (and one VLA).
- [secondary] numbers (Wu 63.6/94.7%, He SDR 0.02/0.31/0.79, Geva 88% MLP knockout) need checking against the full papers.
