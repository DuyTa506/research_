## Q1. Which internal circuits do text reading in VLM decoders (OCR heads, retrieval heads, information flow, attention sinks)?

### Takeaway
Text reading in LVLM decoders is done by a fairly small, identifiable set of attention heads. They attend from the generated token to the image patches being read ("OCR heads", about 10% of heads in one study). These heads are less sparse than text retrieval heads. They overlap strongly with the existing text retrieval/copy heads, and they cluster in the middle-to-late layers (around 47% depth in Qwen3-VL). Masking only 5–20 of them costs up to about 20 F1 on DocVQA. This is a concentrated, fragile circuit, which matters directly for head and layer pruning.

### Cited Findings
- **Baek, Chang, Ryu, Lee, "How Do Large Vision-Language Models See Text in Image? Unveiling the Distinctive Role of OCR Heads"** (arXiv 2505.15865; EMNLP 2025 main, Oral; ACL Anthology 2025.emnlp-main.1032, pp. 20441–20453) [NV]. The authors identify the "Optical Character Recognition Head (OCR Head)" in several LVLMs. Masking the top-N (5, 10, 20) OCR heads causes "significant performance degradation (up to nearly 20% F1 drop)" on OCR-centric VQA (DocVQA, Multi-Page DocVQA). OCR heads are "far less sparse" than previous retrieval heads and behave qualitatively differently from general retrieval heads. How often they activate tracks their OCR scores. The paper also uses CoT prompting, head masking, and redistribution of sink-token values to the OCR heads, which it reports improves performance. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.15865); [ACL Anthology preview](https://preview.aclanthology.org/setup/2025.emnlp-main.1032); [Moonlight review](https://www.themoonlight.io/en/review/how-do-large-vision-language-models-see-text-in-image-unveiling-the-distinctive-role-of-ocr-heads)
- **"From Retrieval to Recognition: How Vision–Language Models Become OCR Specialists"** (arXiv 2609.21543, 2026) [NV]. Visual OCR heads overlap strongly with independently identified text retrieval/copy heads: the untuned top-20 intersection is 73.3% across two general VLMs. The authors conclude that OCR specialization "primarily reweights an existing retrieval-and-copy circuit rather than constructing a new attention-head circuit." — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.21543)
- **"Where Vision Becomes Text: Locating the OCR Routing Bottleneck in Vision-Language Models"** (arXiv 2602.22918, 2026) [NV]. In Qwen3-VL, the point where visual information becomes text-like (the bottleneck) sits at **L17 (about 47% depth) in both the 4B and 8B** models. Ablating the **93 most OCR-selective heads**, which cluster in **L17–24** (selectivity ratio >1.0), **reduces OCR by 8.2 pp while improving CountBench by +5.0 pp**. So OCR heads compete with other visual skills. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.22918v1)
- **"Using OCR Heads to Verbalize Image Semantics"** (arXiv 2609.18823, Bau lab, 2026) [NV]. The authors argue that "OCR heads" are really generic **verbalization heads**. They can read out the semantics of image tokens that contain no text, and they make up about 10% of attention heads across the four models studied. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.18823v1); [project page](https://ocr.baulab.info/)
- **Zhang, Yadav, Han, Shutova, "Cross-modal Information Flow in Multimodal Large Language Models"** (arXiv 2411.18620; CVPR 2025) [NV]. The study uses attention knockout on LLaVA-series models and finds three stages. Lower layers move general whole-image visual features into the question-token positions. Middle layers move object-specific, question-relevant visual information into the question positions. Higher layers move the combined representation to the last position for prediction. This is a short-answer VQA setting, not long transcription. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.18620); [mlanthology CVPR 2025](https://mlanthology.org/cvpr/2025/zhang2025cvpr-crossmodal)
- **Kang, Kim, Kim, Hwang, "See What You Are Told: Visual Attention Sink in Large Multimodal Models"** (arXiv 2503.03321; ICLR 2025) [NV]. LMMs put high attention on irrelevant "visual sink tokens", which are marked by massive activations in a few hidden dimensions. In LLaVA-1.5-7B these activations **emerge at layer 2** from early FFNs and persist through the residual stream. Removing sink tokens does not significantly hurt output quality. Their method, VAR (Visual Attention Redistribution), is training-free and moves attention from sink tokens to relevant image tokens in "image-centric heads". — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.03321); [ICLR proceedings](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/da8a39bc39ae1c89dd6ebb1e3bcbb3f3-Paper-Conference.pdf)

### Inferences
- For LightOnOCR the OCR transcription task is "copy the glyph under the current reading position into the output". That matches the retrieval/copy-head mechanism (overlap of about 73% per 2609.21543). The heads that matter are the ones with high OCR score / retrieval score, and they will be concentrated in a few layers.
- Masking only 5–20 heads costs up to about 20 F1, and ablating 93 heads costs 8.2 pp. So **head-level or layer-level structured pruning that removes even a small number of OCR heads can disproportionately damage transcription**, while general-VQA proxies barely move. The CountBench result even improved by +5. So a calibration or eval set without OCR may not detect the damage.
- Sink tokens and massive activations (from early FFNs at layer 2) are a known structure. Width pruning of early FFN channels that carry the massive-activation dimensions could change the attention budget of the OCR heads. This is an inference only, untested in the literature found.
- Practical diagnostic for the pruned model: score every head for OCR/retrieval (attention from output token t to the image patch containing the ground-truth glyph). Compare teacher vs pruned student, and treat OCR-head survival or recovery as a distillation target, e.g. attention-map distillation for those heads.

### Gaps
- I could not access the exact OCR-head scoring formula, the model list, the layer distribution, or per-dataset masking tables from Baek et al. (ACL Anthology and arXiv blocked).
- I found no interpretability paper studying OCR heads specifically in **long-form page transcription** (1,000+ tokens); all of them use VQA-style short answers.
- The other papers suggested in the brief were not retrieved in this pass: "What's in the image?" (2411.17491), "Towards interpreting visual information processing in VLMs" (2410.07149), and the logit-lens paper 2410.02762 (Jiang et al.). I have no verified numbers for them.

## Q2. Vision encoder: sensitivity to glyph size and resolution, and the effect of shrinking the ViT

### Takeaway
Text reading is far more sensitive to ViT size and input resolution than general VQA. In RADIOv2.5 (LLaVA-1.5 + Vicuna-7B), going from ViT-H to ViT-B at 768px drops TextVQA (no OCR tokens) by **12.97 pts (56.93 → 43.96)**, but GQA-val by only **2.05 pts (74.04 → 71.99)**. Raising ViT-B's resolution from 432 to 768 adds **+11.53** on TextVQA-no-tokens and only **+1.50** on GQA-val. Compression ratio (vision tokens per text token) sets a hard ceiling on OCR precision: DeepSeek-OCR reports about 97% at ≤10× and about 60% at about 20×.

- **RADIO README** (GitHub) — READ IN FULL. C-RADIOv2-VLM powers Llama Nemotron Nano VL 8B, which the README calls "currently #1 on OCR Bench v2" (6.25.2025 entry; no score given). There are no OCR tables in the README. — [RADIO README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NVlabs/RADIO/main/README.md)
- **DeepSeek-OCR: Contexts Optical Compression** (arXiv 2510.18234, 2025) [NV]. On the Fox benchmark English documents with 600–1,300 text tokens (about 100 pages), the paper reports "96%+ OCR decoding precision at 9–10× text compression, ∼90% at 10–12× compression, and ∼60% at 20× compression". — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.18234). A secondary source (36Kr) attributes the drop beyond 10× to layout complexity and blurring of long text at 512×512 / 640×640 resolution. This is secondary interpretation. — [36Kr](https://eu.36kr.com/en/p/3517473609718916)
- **Goh et al. 2021 "Multimodal Neurons in Artificial Neural Networks" (distill.pub)** — not retrieved (distill.pub ENOTFOUND). No numbers included.

### Inferences
- For LightOnOCR's 12-block ViT: **narrowing or removing ViT blocks is likely to hurt transcription much more than general-VQA metrics would suggest.** The RADIO numbers show text representation scaling steeply with encoder capacity (relative loss about 8× larger on TextVQA-NoTok than on GQA). If the ViT is pruned, raising input resolution or token count can partly compensate (B-768 ≈ L-432), at a compute cost.
- Pages with about 1,500 output tokens put LightOnOCR's text-token-to-vision-token ratio well inside the regime where DeepSeek-OCR shows a sharp precision cliff (>10×). Any compression that effectively lowers visual resolution, such as token merging, fewer patches, or a weaker ViT, pushes toward that cliff. Small fonts and dense pages will fail first.

### Gaps
- I could not verify the dedicated "small text / font size" VLM papers ("Do VLMs see small text?", TextMonkey, LLaVA-UHD, DocOwl resolution ablations) within the tool budget. I have no numbers for them.
- Materzynska et al. 2022 ("Disentangling visual and written concepts in CLIP", CVPR) was not retrieved (CVF blocked).
- RADIO results are for LLaVA-1.5 on TextVQA, not dense page OCR. The size of the gap for full-page transcription is unmeasured.

## Q3. What breaks under compression: weight pruning, quantization, and visual token pruning on OCR-heavy benchmarks

### Takeaway
The clearest evidence of disproportionate OCR damage comes from **visual token pruning**. At about 160 retained tokens, DocVQA and OCR fall to 15–30 for FastV, SparseVLM, and VisionZip (versus 39–57 at about 640 tokens). OCRBench and DocVQA are repeatedly called the most budget-sensitive benchmarks, while TextVQA is less sensitive. For **structured weight pruning**, Huang et al. (IJCV 2026) find that layerwise pruning hurts complex tasks, including DocVQA, more than widthwise pruning. Widthwise wins at high compression with recovery. I found no OCR-specific head-pruning study. For quantization, the evidence only points qualitatively to 2–3-bit collapse on suites that include OCR.

### Cited Findings
- **LLMC+: Benchmarking Vision-Language Model Compression** (arXiv 2508.09981, 2025) [NV]. DocVQA / OCR scores by retained tokens: at about 640 tokens, FastV 46.6 / 38.9, SparseVLM 49.1 / 40.6, VisionZip 56.9 / 48.5. At about 160 tokens, FastV 17.8 / 15.4, SparseVLM 17.3 / 18.6, VisionZip 28.8 / 29.5. The paper also argues that question-dependent pruning (FastV, SparseVLM) removes tokens needed in later dialogue turns. That matters for long generation, where the "question" (the next glyph to read) changes at every step. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.09981). Model and base unconfirmed from the snippet.
- **"Rethinking Visual Token Reduction in LVLMs Under Cross-Modal Misalignment"** (AAAI 2026) [NV]. On TextVQA at a moderate budget, retention is SparseVLM-v1.5 98.64%, VisionZip 98.11%, FastV 88.45%. SparseVLM degrades under extreme compression. — [AAAI](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/39949/43910)
- At 94.4% reduction on LLaVA-NeXT-7B, retained performance is FastV 74.7%, SparseVLM 74.5%, VisionZip 82.3% (averaged across benchmarks; source paper in the search set unclear, likely 2509.01552 or 2604.11530) [NV]. — [Variation-aware Vision Token Dropping](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.01552)
- Another study reports that TextVQA is less sensitive to token allocation than DocVQA, which is more sensitive at low budgets [NV; exact source within the result set not pinned down]. — [Beyond Attention Scores (2604.11530)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.11530)
- **"When Token Pruning is Worse than Random"** (arXiv 2512.07580; CVPR 2026) [NV]. The paper notes that OCRBench is particularly sensitive to aggressive visual token pruning. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.07580v2); [CVPR supp.](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/supplemental/Wang_When_Token_Pruning_CVPR_2026_supplemental.pdf)
- Several 2026 papers target the OCR-specific failure of token pruning directly: "Beyond Accuracy: Auditing Spatial Provenance in Visual Token Pruning for OCR-Critical MLLM Inference" (2608.00077), "ET-Prune: Evidence-Aware Dynamic Budgeting for Visual Token Pruning in Text-Rich MLLMs" (2608.01979), and "Nüwa: Mending the Spatial Integrity Torn by VLM Token Pruning" (2602.02951). Their existence shows the community treats text-rich inputs as the worst case for token pruning. I have no numbers for them [NV]. — [2608.00077](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.00077); [2608.01979](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.01979); [2602.02951](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.02951v1)
- **Huang et al., "Structural Pruning of Large Vision Language Models: A Comprehensive Study on Pruning Dynamics, Recovery, and Data Efficiency"** (arXiv 2604.24380; IJCV 2026 extended version of arXiv 2507.20749 / GCPR 2025 Oral) [NV]. Layerwise pruning "disproportionately degrades performance on complex reasoning tasks" compared with widthwise, with the largest gap on MathVista, DocVQA, and GQA. Without fine-tuning, widthwise beats layerwise, e.g. AI2D 65.64 vs 52.73. With recovery, layerwise is slightly better at low compression and widthwise is better at high compression. The crossover is reported at about 30% in one version and about 40% in another; versions disagree. — [arXiv 2604.24380](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.24380); [Springer IJCV](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11263-026-02865-5); [GitHub](https://github.com/YiranHuangIrene/VLMCompression); [2507.20749](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.20749)
- **Quantization** [NV]. VLMQ (arXiv 2508.03351) evaluates INT3/INT2 on ChartQA, DocVQA, OCRBench, and TextVQA. Its pilot study on DocVQA probes the role of vision tokens under INT3. SPEED-Q (arXiv 2511.08914) reports that "nearly all existing methods suffer severe accuracy degradation under 2-bit quantization" on suites including OCRBench, while 4-bit stays close to full precision. I found no per-benchmark ranking showing OCR to be the most quantization-sensitive. — [VLMQ](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.03351v2); [SPEED-Q](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.08914)

### Inferences
- The token-pruning results are the strongest evidence that **OCR quality depends on keeping fine-grained, spatially complete visual information available to the decoder at every generation step**. Attention-score-based pruning (FastV) is worst, because attention concentrates on sinks and salient regions, not on the glyph currently being read. For LightOnOCR, any change that reduces how many image tokens the decoder can retrieve from is a high-risk change.
- For weight pruning, Huang et al. report the layerwise vs widthwise trade-off, and OCR heads cluster in specific layers (Q1). Together these suggest **layer dropping is the most dangerous structured choice for OCR**: dropping one layer can remove an entire OCR-head cluster. Width pruning of heads is safer only if OCR/retrieval heads are explicitly protected by importance scoring on OCR data. This is an inference; no paper tested it directly.
- Calibration and importance data for pruning should be OCR transcription data. General VQA calibration would under-rate OCR heads (see the CountBench +5 / OCR −8.2 trade-off in Q1).

### Gaps
- I found **no paper that prunes heads in a VLM decoder and reports OCR-specific breakage**. The closest is Baek et al.'s masking (Q1).
- I have no verified per-benchmark TextVQA/DocVQA/OCRBench numbers from Huang et al. 2604.24380 (full text blocked). Whether its DocVQA drop exceeds GQA's under layerwise pruning could not be confirmed.
- The original FastV, SparseVLM, and VisionZip paper tables (TextVQA vs GQA retention at 64/128/192 tokens) were not retrieved. The numbers above come from third-party benchmarks with unknown base models.

## Q4. Hybrid linear-attention / SSM VLMs: which layers carry image-to-text copying, and OCR weaknesses

### Takeaway
Consistent indirect evidence says linear-attention and SSM layers are weak at the fine-grained retrieval that OCR needs. Pure Mamba VLMs trail Transformers on text-rich tasks. InfiniteVL states outright that "linear attention underperforms on OCR and document understanding", and its design hands fine-grained detail to sliding-window (softmax) attention layers. **No paper found mechanistically attributes image→text copying to the full-attention layers of a hybrid VLM.** The inference that LightOnOCR's about 6 full-attention layers host the OCR/copy heads is plausible but unverified.

### Cited Findings
- **InfiniteVL: Synergizing Linear and Sparse Attention** (arXiv 2512.08829, 2025) [NV]. The abstract says linear attention underperforms on OCR and document understanding, and that window-based VLMs degrade once sequence length exceeds the window. Gated DeltaNet is combined with sliding-window attention (SWA). The model card credits the SWA layers with "high-resolution local context and fine-grained visual detail" for information-intensive OCR and document tasks, and Gated DeltaNet with long-term memory. The base model keeps a small fraction of full-attention layers interleaved with Gated DeltaNet. The layer ratio is inconsistent across secondary sources (one says 1 SWA : 3 GDN, window 8192). — [HF paper page](https://huggingface.co/papers/2512.08829); [HF model card](https://huggingface.co/hustvl/InfiniteVL); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.08829)
- **Cobra** (arXiv 2403.14520, 2024) [NV]. In Cobra's TextVQA prompt analysis, placing OCR tokens after the question hurt most models, which the authors attribute to "the inherent inductive bias of the RNN models". — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.14520)
- **TextVQA numbers for Mamba VLMs are inconsistent across papers** [NV]. MaTVLM's table lists Cobra at 57.9. Another comparison lists Cobra 46.0 / VL-Mamba 48.9. Zamba2-VL lists Cobra-3.5B at 58.2. Mamba-VL 2.8B is at 42.1 in one comparison. Compare only within a paper. — [MaTVLM arXiv 2503.13440](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.13440); [Zamba2-VL 2606.00390](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.00390v1)
- **MaTVLM: Hybrid Mamba-Transformer for Efficient VLM** (arXiv 2503.13440; ICCV 2025) [NV]. The model replaces a fraction of the TinyLLaVA decoder's attention layers with Mamba-2. The 25% hybrid scores **57.7 on TextVQA**, versus Cobra 57.9 in the same table. Teacher numbers were not retrieved. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.13440)
- **LinMU** (arXiv 2601.01322, 2026) [NV]. Most linear-complexity VLMs replace all attention layers with Mamba or RWKV and "often lag behind Transformer-based VLMs on complex vision understanding tasks". The paper attributes this partly to an "adjacency preservation issue" (2D neighbours in the image become distant in the 1D scan). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.01322)
- **mmMamba** (arXiv 2502.13145, 2025) [NV]. The method distills a Transformer VLM into Mamba-2 in fully linear or hybrid form (some attention layers kept) and reports TextVQA against Cobra-3.5B. No numbers retrieved. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.13145)
- **Nemotron-H** (arXiv 2504.03624, 2025) [NV]. The hybrid Mamba-Transformer VLM variant reports **OCRBench ≈836–869**, compared with Qwen2.5 / NVLM-based baselines. So a hybrid with a few attention layers can be OCR-competitive. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.03624)
- **"Shaking Up VLMs: Comparing Transformers and Structured State Space Models"** (Pantazopoulos et al., EMNLP 2024 main, 2024.emnlp-main.793) [NV; ACL Anthology blocked, so no numbers extracted]. This is a controlled Mamba vs Transformer VLM comparison on captioning, QA, and grounding/retrieval. — [ACL Anthology PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.793.pdf)

### Inferences
- Combining Q1 and Q4: OCR is a retrieval-and-copy circuit (OCR heads overlap retrieval heads by about 73%). Linear-attention and SSM layers have a fixed-size state and are known to underperform on OCR and document tasks. So **in LightOnOCR the image→text copying is most likely concentrated in the about 6 full-attention layers.** The GatedDeltaNet layers most likely provide local and sequential context: position along the line, language modelling, layout state. This is an inference, not a measured result.
- Consequence for pruning to 0.3B / 0.1B: **dropping or heavily narrowing any of the full-attention layers (or their heads / KV dimensions) is probably the single most damaging operation for transcription.** Pruning GatedDeltaNet layers or width is likely less harmful to glyph copying, but may hurt reading order and line tracking over 1,500 tokens. Recommended check: per-layer ablation on the teacher (zero out each full-attention layer vs each GDN layer, measure CER on pages), and per-head OCR scores inside the full-attention layers.
- Cobra's finding (prompt order matters for RNN models) suggests that in GDN layers, information about image tokens must survive in the recurrent state across 1,000+ generated tokens. That makes full-attention layers even more essential for re-reading the page at each step.

### Gaps
- I found **no mechanistic interpretability study** of OCR or retrieval heads in a hybrid GatedDeltaNet/Mamba + attention VLM, including Qwen3.5-VL-style, Nemotron Nano VL, VisualRWKV, ML-Mamba, and VL-Mamba. No paper shows directly that full-attention layers carry image→text copying in hybrids.
- I could not retrieve OCRBench or DocVQA numbers for Cobra, VL-Mamba, or ML-Mamba. The TextVQA numbers conflict across papers.
- I have no per-layer ablation data for any hybrid OCR model, including LightOnOCR itself. This would need to be measured experimentally.
