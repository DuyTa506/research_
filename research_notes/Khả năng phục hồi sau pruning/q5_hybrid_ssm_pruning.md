# Q5: Structured pruning of hybrid linear-attention / SSM decoders: what can be removed and how it recovers

## KQ1: Which papers prune Mamba/Mamba-2, RWKV, (Gated)DeltaNet, and hybrids structurally, and what are the key numbers?

### Takeaway
The most mature work on structured pruning of hybrids is NVIDIA's Minitron/Puzzle line on Mamba-2 hybrids: Nemotron-H 56B→47B, Minitron-SSM 8B→4B, Nemotron Nano 2 12B→9B, Nemotron Elastic and Nemotron-Labs-3-Puzzle. These papers prune Mamba heads, but not the channels within a head. They also prune FFN width, embedding width and depth, and they recover with logit distillation on roughly 60–400B tokens. For pure SSMs, the structured option is block or SSM-module removal (Mamba-Shedder). The one-shot weight methods (SparseSSM, unstructured pruning) do not give structured speedups. I found no paper that structurally prunes Gated DeltaNet heads. The closest is SlimQwen, which compresses Qwen3-Next.

### Cited Findings

**Nemotron-H (arXiv 2504.03624, NVIDIA, Apr 2025; OpenReview listing)**: MiniPuzzle 56B→47B
- The 56B model has 118 layers, 10 of which are self-attention. The 47B model keeps 5 self-attention layers (half), 44 Mamba-2 layers (down from 54) and 49 FFN layers (down from 54). The FFN intermediate size was also pruned. — [arXiv 2504.03624](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.03624); [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.03624v2) (via search-result text)
- MiniPuzzle combines lightweight pruning, NAS and distillation. It first collects importance scores per component (e.g. per layer). One source reports that some self-attention layers ranked among the least important, notably the 84th layer, which is the 7th self-attention layer. — [arXiv 2504.03624](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.03624); [Medium summary (secondary)](https://ritvik19.medium.com/papers-explained-453-nemotron-h-bc40f4b899cb)
- Distillation used only 63B tokens with FP8 training. The 47B-Base model matches the 56B on accuracy and runs about 20% faster (1.2×). — [arXiv 2504.03624](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.03624); [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=Bj5GqAJBtB)

**Minitron-SSM: "Efficient Hybrid Language Model Compression through Group-Aware SSM Pruning"** (Taghibakhshi et al., arXiv 2504.11409, NeurIPS 2025): verified as the right paper
- It compresses Nemotron-H 8B to 4B using group-aware Mamba pruning, together with FFN, embedding and layer pruning, followed by knowledge distillation. — [mlanthology NeurIPS 2025](https://mlanthology.org/neurips/2025/taghibakhshi2025neurips-efficient/); [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.11409); [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=m3huAdsaGI)
- The 4B model keeps more than 96% of the 8B model's accuracy and uses up to 40× fewer training tokens than similarly sized models. — [Bytez summary](https://bytez.com/docs/arxiv/2504.11409/paper); [mlanthology](https://mlanthology.org/neurips/2025/taghibakhshi2025neurips-efficient/)
- The reported speedup conflicts across sources: about 2× faster inference in the mlanthology abstract, about 1.4× in the Liner review. This is unresolved. — [mlanthology](https://mlanthology.org/neurips/2025/taghibakhshi2025neurips-efficient/); [Liner (secondary)](https://liner.com/review/efficient-hybrid-language-model-compression-through-groupaware-ssm-pruning)
- Pruning whole Mamba heads gave better speed and lower LM loss than pruning head channels (secondary). "Group-aware" means the pruning respects Mamba-2's grouped B/C structure and head structure. — [Liner (secondary)](https://liner.com/review/efficient-hybrid-language-model-compression-through-groupaware-ssm-pruning)
- At a fixed compression ratio, width-only pruning beat depth-only pruning (secondary, not verified against the paper). — [Moonlight review](https://www.themoonlight.io/en/review/efficient-hybrid-language-model-compression-through-group-aware-ssm-pruning)
- The released Nemotron-H-4B-Instruct-128K scores 7.9 on MT-Bench and 76.24 on IFEval (strict average). — [HF model card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/Nemotron-H-4B-Instruct-128K)

**NVIDIA Nemotron Nano 2 (arXiv 2508.14444, Aug 2025)**: 12B→9B
- The search space covered depth, embedding channels, FFN dimension and Mamba heads. Depth went from 62 to 56 layers. — [HF blog (NVIDIA)](https://huggingface.co/blog/nvidia/supercharge-ai-reasoning-with-nemotron-nano-2); [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.14444)
- The final model has 56 layers, 4 of them attention. Embedding width went from 5120 to 4480 and FFN width from 20480 to 15680 (secondary summary). I did not find the final Mamba head count. — [Emergent Mind (secondary)](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/nemotron-nano-9b-v2)
- Recovery used logit distillation with forward KL and the 12B as teacher. A short distillation run picked the best architecture candidate, then a longer run produced the final model. Compression was applied after the 12B had been aligned (SFT, GRPO, DPO, RLHF). — [arXiv 2508.14444](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.14444); [HF blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/nvidia/supercharge-ai-reasoning-with-nemotron-nano-2)
- NVIDIA's Minitron repo says Minitron-SSM compresses Mamba2 layers in hybrid LLMs and was used to create Nemotron Nano v2 9B. A worked recipe is published in Model-Optimizer. — [NVlabs/minitron](https://github.com/nvlabs/minitron); [Model-Optimizer README](https://github.com/NVIDIA/Model-Optimizer/blob/main/examples/pruning/minitron/NVIDIA-Nemotron-Nano-9B-v2/README.md)

**Nemotron Elastic (arXiv 2511.16664)**: nested 12B/9B/6B in one hybrid Mamba-2/attention/MLP model
- Router networks map a budget to width and depth decisions, which are turned into masks with Gumbel-Softmax. The 6B and 9B variants needed about 110B tokens (65B short-context plus 45B extended-context), distilled from the 12B parent. — [arXiv 2511.16664](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.16664); [HF model card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/Nemotron-Elastic-12B); [MarkTechPost (secondary)](https://www.marktechpost.com/2025/11/23/nvidia-ai-releases-nemotron-elastic-12b-a-single-ai-model-that-gives-you-6b-9b-12b-variants-without-extra-training-cost/)
- Nemotron 3 Nano 4B was pruned and distilled from Nano 9B v2 with this framework, and the router chose depth and Mamba-head pruning. A follow-up, Star Elastic (arXiv 2605.07182), extends the method to hybrid Mamba-Attention-MoE models such as Nemotron Nano v3 30B. — [HF blog: Nemotron 3 Nano 4B](https://huggingface.co/blog/nvidia/nemotron-3-nano-4b); [arXiv 2605.07182](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.07182)

**Nemotron-Labs-3-Puzzle-75B-A9B (arXiv 2607.04371, Jul 2026)**: hybrid MoE, 120.7B→75.3B total parameters (12.8B→9.3B active)
- No layers were removed. The 88-block layout (40 Mamba, 40 MoE, 8 attention) is kept. The SSM state size in Mamba layers went from 128 to 96, applied uniformly because of inference-framework limitations. Attention layers were left unchanged because the parent was already KV-cache-efficient. The MoE top-k budget was cut to 50% and allocated unevenly across layers. — [arXiv HTML 2607.04371](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.04371v1); [HF model card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/NVIDIA-Nemotron-Labs-3-Puzzle-75B-A9B-NVFP4)
- A secondary report gives 2.03× the server throughput of Nemotron 3 Super. — [remio (secondary)](https://www.remio.ai/post/nvidia-releases-nemotron-labs-3-puzzle-75b-a9b-compressed-hybrid-moe-model-with-2-03x-server-through)

**Mamba-Shedder (arXiv 2501.17088, NAACL 2025)**: training-free structured removal in Mamba, Mamba-2 and hybrids
- It removes whole Mamba blocks, SSM modules only, or MLP channels. It reports up to 1.4× inference speedup with minimal quality loss. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.naacl-long.195/); [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.17088v1)
- **Mamba2-2.7B** has 64 SSM modules. Removing 24 gives up to 1.18× decode and 1.20× prefill speedup. Removing 16 costs about 0.4% accuracy and +0.16 perplexity (secondary). Mamba-2 tolerates SSM-module removal better than Mamba-1. — [ACL PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2025.naacl-long.195.pdf); [Liner (secondary)](https://liner.com/review/mambashedder-posttransformer-compression-for-efficient-selective-structured-state-space-models)
- **Zamba2-2.7B (hybrid)**: the best result combined removing blocks, MLP channels and SSM modules. Removing about 18 of 54 SSM modules kept perplexity at 5.18 and average accuracy at 65.9 (10.27% compression), with a 1.39× decode speedup. Hybrids were reported to be more sensitive to whole-block pruning, so finer-grained removal worked better (secondary). — [ACL PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2025.naacl-long.195.pdf); [Emergent Mind (secondary)](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/mamba-shedder)
- **Hymba-1.5B**: removing 7 blocks gives 1.24× decode and 1.15× prefill speedup. Sources disagree on whether that is 7 of 32 or 7 of 64 blocks. — [ACL PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2025.naacl-long.195.pdf)
- Recovery tuning raised average accuracy to 63.7 in one configuration (secondary, configuration unclear). — [ACL PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2025.naacl-long.195.pdf)

**SparseSSM (Tuo & Wang, arXiv 2506.09613, listed as an ICML 2026 poster)**: one-shot OBS pruning of Mamba SSM weights. This is unstructured or semi-structured weight sparsity, not head or channel removal.
- It is the first OBS-based, training-free pruning for selective SSMs, and it accounts for the time-shared, discretized state-transition matrix. The reported result is 50% of SSM weights pruned without fine-tuning and no zero-shot accuracy loss (aggregator-reported). It was tested on Mamba-130M through 1.4B. — [arXiv 2506.09613](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09613v1); [ICML 2026 poster](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/poster/64442); [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=bzb9OJVfCP)

**Other pure-SSM pruning work**
- PerfMamba (arXiv 2511.22849) proposes Δ-guided structured pruning of the SSM state dimension on Mamba2-130M. It finds the SSM accounts for 60–70% of runtime and memory, and that throughput and memory improve while accuracy holds "within a moderate pruning regime". — [arXiv 2511.22849](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.22849)
- Shihab et al., "Efficient Unstructured Pruning of Mamba SSMs" (arXiv 2505.08299, EMNLP 2025), is unstructured. It reaches up to 70% parameter reduction with more than 95% performance retained and adds an eigenvalue-stability check for the recurrent dynamics. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.562/)

**Gated DeltaNet / Qwen3-Next**
- SlimQwen (arXiv 2605.08738) compresses Qwen3-Next-80A3B to a 23A2B model (about 4×) with continual pretraining. With the same training tokens, progressive pruning schedules beat one-shot compression. The snippets did not show whether it prunes Gated DeltaNet heads or only experts and layers, so this needs a check. — [arXiv 2605.08738](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08738)
- I found no other paper on structured pruning of GatedDeltaNet or DeltaNet heads, key/value dimensions or layers (see Gaps).

### Inferences
- The recipe best supported by evidence for a Mamba-2-style hybrid has four steps: (1) score heads, FFN neurons, embedding channels and layers on a calibration set; (2) prune whole SSM heads with group awareness, not channels within heads; (3) favour width over depth at moderate ratios; (4) recover with logit-KD from the unpruned parent, including a short KD probe to rank candidates. It maps naturally onto GatedDeltaNet, whose heads are independent recurrences with their own q/k/v/gates. Grouped K-heads versus V-heads would be the "group-aware" constraint there: Qwen3-Next has 16 Q/K heads and 32 V heads.
- NVIDIA's ratios are mild (about 1.2× to 2×). The user's 0.8B→0.3B (about 2.7×) and 0.8B→0.1B (about 8×) targets go well beyond any published hybrid-pruning point. Minitron-SSM's 2× with more than 96% retention is the largest published ratio I found.

### Gaps
- I found no structured pruning of RWKV (any version), DeltaNet or GatedDeltaNet heads or state, Jamba, Samba, Falcon-H1, MiniMax-01 or Kimi Linear.
- I could not get the per-axis sensitivity tables from Minitron-SSM (head versus channel versus state dim versus layers) or the per-benchmark tables, because arXiv was blocked.
- Mamba-Shedder's Hymba block count (7 of 32 versus 7 of 64) is unresolved.

## KQ2: Do linear-attention/SSM layers and full-attention layers differ in how well they recover after removal, and do full-attention layers carry retrieval and copying?

### Takeaway
Yes. Across several independent sources, the few full-attention layers or heads in a hybrid carry almost all exact retrieval and copying. Removing them drives retrieval to about 0%, and the SSM layers do not compensate. Yet only a small, specific subset of attention heads or layers is needed: about 2–3 layers, about 15% of heads, or about 2% of heads in distilled hybrids. SSM and linear layers are individually more redundant: many can be dropped (Mamba-Shedder), and NVIDIA prunes Mamba heads freely. One caution: NVIDIA's MiniPuzzle did drop 5 of 10 attention layers in Nemotron-H-56B, and Puzzle-75B kept all attention unchanged. Which attention layers stay should therefore be chosen with a retrieval-sensitive metric, not just LM loss or MMLU.

### Cited Findings
- **"Some Attention is All You Need for Retrieval"** (Michalak & Abreu, arXiv 2510.19861, Oct 2025): ablating attention in RecurrentGemma-2B/9B and Jamba-Mini-1.6 drives retrieval accuracy to 0%, and the SSM layers show no compensation even with better prompting. Keeping only 15% of attention heads (selected by lowest entropy) preserves near-perfect retrieval and 84% of MMLU performance. The paper states two hypotheses: functional exclusivity (retrieval depends only on attention) and retrieval specialization. — [arXiv 2510.19861](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.19861)
- **Bick, Xing & Gu, "Understanding the Skill Gap in Recurrent LMs: The Role of the Gather-and-Aggregate Mechanism"** (arXiv 2504.18574, ICML 2025): retrieval in both Transformers and SSMs runs through a Gather-and-Aggregate (G&A) circuit concentrated in a few heads. Pretrained hybrids assign the Aggregate role to their attention layers. In a distilled SSM, replacing the single layer where Aggregate heads emerge with attention raised MMLU from 33% to 50%. — [PMLR v267](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/bick25a.html); [arXiv 2504.18574](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.18574)
- **Retrieval-Aware Distillation (RAD) for Transformer-SSM Hybrids** (Bick et al., arXiv 2602.11374, ICML 2026 poster): heads are ranked by ablating each one on a synthetic retrieval task. Only the top G&A heads are kept as attention; the rest are distilled into recurrent heads using MOHAWK on Llama-3.2-1B and Qwen2.5-1.5B. Keeping 10 heads (about 2% of 512) recovers more than 95% of teacher performance on retrieval-heavy tasks. Compared with an SSM-only student, SWDE rises from 27.7% to 71.1% and KV-Retrieval from 13.2% to 99%. The model also uses a reduced SSM state dimension (d=8) and is reported as 5–6× more memory-efficient than comparable hybrids. Sources disagree on how much attention competing hybrids retain: "≥25%" in the arXiv abstract, "30–50%" in the ICML abstract. — [arXiv 2602.11374](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11374); [ICML 2026](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/poster/65938)
- **Jet-Nemotron / PostNAS** (arXiv 2508.15884, NeurIPS 2025): starting from Qwen2.5, the MLPs are frozen and a once-for-all supernet is trained in which each layer can be full or linear attention. Beam search then places full attention under a budget. For retrieval, only 2–3 layers are critical. For MMLU, only 2 layers are critical, and they are not the same layers as for retrieval. PostNAS placement clearly beats uniform placement. Jet-Nemotron-2B (from Qwen2.5-1.5B) keeps 2 full-attention layers and Jet-Nemotron-4B (from Qwen2.5-3B) keeps 3. The layer indices conflict across secondary sources; Table 10 of the paper has them. — [NeurIPS 2025 paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/4364fbe773a77fb373df5c7f1d4cbb53-Paper-Conference.pdf); [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.15884v1); [GitHub](https://github.com/NVlabs/Jet-Nemotron)
- **The Mamba in the Llama** (arXiv 2408.15237, NeurIPS 2024): attention layers from Llama-3 are replaced by Mamba blocks initialised from attention weights, keeping 50%, 25% or 12.5% attention. Quality degrades as the attention fraction shrinks. The 50%-attention models are close to the teacher on chat benchmarks, and the 50% and 25% hybrids get more than 1.8× speedup on an RTX 3090. — [arXiv 2408.15237](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.15237); [GitHub](https://github.com/jxiw/MambaInLlama)
- **Llamba / MOHAWK** (arXiv 2502.14458): a pure Mamba-2 model distilled from Llama-3.x with the three MOHAWK stages (matrix orientation, hidden-state alignment, weight transfer plus KD), using less than 0.1% of the training data of comparable models. In the Zebra-Llama comparison table, Llamba scores 63.44 (column not identified). — [arXiv 2502.14458](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.14458v1); [Zebra-Llama arXiv 2505.17272](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.17272v1)
- **LightTransfer** (arXiv 2410.13846): "lazy" attention layers attend mostly to the initial and recent tokens. Replacing them with streaming attention, even when half the layers are lazy, gives up to 2.17× throughput with less than 1.5% LongBench loss and no training for long-context understanding. This shows only part of the full-attention layers need global attention. — [arXiv 2410.13846](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13846)
- **NVIDIA hybrid compression keeps few attention layers but does not prune them all**: Nemotron-H 56B→47B went from 10 to 5 attention layers, with some attention layers among the least important by MiniPuzzle score. Nano 2 9B keeps 4 attention layers in 56. Puzzle-75B left all 8 attention blocks untouched and pruned Mamba state 128→96 instead. — [arXiv 2504.03624](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.03624); [Emergent Mind (secondary)](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/nemotron-nano-9b-v2); [arXiv 2607.04371](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.04371v1)
- **Recoverability of SSM layers**: Mamba-2 tolerates removing 16–24 of 64 SSM modules training-free with small loss, but hybrids (Zamba2) are more sensitive to whole-block removal than to finer-grained removal. — [ACL PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2025.naacl-long.195.pdf)

### Inferences
- For OCR, generation is long and has to copy faithfully from image tokens, so it is a retrieval/copy workload. This evidence suggests the gated full-attention layers in the LightOnOCR decoder (about 1 in 4 if it follows Qwen3.5) are the last thing to remove. A safer plan is to keep all of them, or at least the most retrieval-critical ones, and prune them by heads or KV width rather than depth. Rank them with a copy/retrieval probe, such as OCR transcription loss or a synthetic copy task. LM loss or MMLU alone should not be used, since Jet-Nemotron shows MMLU-critical and retrieval-critical layers differ.
- The GatedDeltaNet layers (about 3 in 4) are the natural target for depth and head pruning, with KD recovery. Small-model evidence (RAD: d=8 state and 2% attention heads is enough) suggests the recurrent state can be shrunk aggressively if a few attention heads stay intact. This is an inference from Mamba-family results, not from GatedDeltaNet.
- With ~0.1B targets, the evidence (RAD, PostNAS) favours keeping a small but non-zero number of full-attention layers. Dropping to an attention-free decoder is likely to break long copying unless retrieval heads are explicitly preserved.

### Gaps
- No study ablates attention versus GatedDeltaNet layers in Qwen3-Next or Qwen3.5 specifically, or in an OCR/VLM hybrid.
- The Jamba (arXiv 2403.19887) ablation, where pure Mamba reportedly fails in-context format adherence and the paper uses a 1:7 attention:Mamba ratio, is unverified (prior knowledge). Search did not surface it.
- "Repeat After Me: Transformers are Better than SSMs at Copying" (Jelassi et al., arXiv 2402.01032, ICML 2024) is unverified (prior knowledge). It is relevant to OCR copying but was not checked here.
- "Attention Amnesia in Hybrid LLMs: When CoT Fine-Tuning Breaks Long-Range Recall" (arXiv 2606.11052) surfaced as a title only. Its content is unverified. It may be relevant: fine-tuning or KD of hybrids can degrade long-range recall.
- I found no evidence on whether pruned attention layers recover with KD as well as pruned SSM layers do (a controlled comparison).

## KQ3: Architecture facts (Qwen3-Next/Qwen3.5 layout) and other hybrids/distillation context

### Takeaway
Qwen3-Next and Qwen3.5 use a 3:1 pattern of Gated DeltaNet to gated (softmax) attention. Qwen3-Next-80B-A3B has 48 layers (36 GDN, 12 attention). The Qwen3.5-397B-A17B variant has 45 GDN and 15 attention layers. The head configuration of small Qwen3.5 models, and so of a LightOnOCR-3-0.8B decoder, was not verified.

### Cited Findings
- Qwen3-Next-80B-A3B has 48 layers in 12 repeats of [3× Gated DeltaNet + 1× gated attention], i.e. 36 GDN and 12 attention layers. Gated attention has 16 Q heads, 2 KV heads and head dim 256. Gated DeltaNet has 16 Q/K heads, 32 V heads and head dim 128 (blog analyses; primary is the Qwen blog/config). — [Alibaba Cloud blog: Qwen3-Next](https://www.alibabacloud.com/blog/602580); [Raschka: Hybrid Attention](https://sebastianraschka.com/llm-architecture-gallery/hybrid-attention/); [mlabonne HF blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/mlabonne/qwen35)
- Gated attention multiplies the attention output by a sigmoid gate computed from the query projection. — [Raschka: Gated Attention](https://sebastianraschka.com/llm-architecture-gallery/gated-attention/); [qiuzh20/gated_attention](https://github.com/qiuzh20/gated_attention)
- Qwen3.5 keeps the 3:1 pattern. The 397B-A17B model has 45 GDN and 15 attention layers. — [Raschka gallery](https://sebastianraschka.com/llm-architecture-gallery/hybrid-attention/)
- The Qwen team reports that Gated DeltaNet gives stronger in-context learning than sliding-window attention or Mamba2. — [Alibaba Cloud blog](https://www.alibabacloud.com/blog/602580)

### Inferences
- Because GDN has twice as many V heads as Q/K heads (in the 80B config), head pruning in GDN probably has to remove V-head groups together with their shared Q/K head. This mirrors the "group-aware" constraint of Minitron-SSM for Mamba-2 groups. The constraint has to be confirmed on the actual LightOnOCR config.

### Gaps
- The exact LightOnOCR-3-0.8B decoder config (layers, GDN and attention head counts, whether it is Qwen3.5-0.8B-based) was not verified. The project's config.json should be checked.
- The following were not verified in this session (prior knowledge, unverified): Kimi Linear (arXiv 2510.26692; 3:1 KDA:MLA), MiniMax-01 (arXiv 2501.08313; 7 lightning-attention : 1 softmax), Falcon-H1 (arXiv 2507.22448; parallel attention + Mamba-2), Hymba (arXiv 2411.13676; parallel heads), Samba (arXiv 2406.07522), Zamba (arXiv 2405.16712), LoLCATs (arXiv 2410.10254). I found no structured-pruning results for any of them.
- I did not obtain Zebra-Llama's (arXiv 2505.17272) own numbers.
