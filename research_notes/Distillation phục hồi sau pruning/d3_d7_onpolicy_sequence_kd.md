## D3: On-policy distillation (OPD) for pruned/compressed models: gain over off-policy at equal compute, and does it help long outputs more?

### Takeaway
A cluster of 2026 papers (ShortOPD, LOOP, ReCal, Fragility of pruning-recovery) treats OPD with the unpruned model as a frozen teacher as the strongest recovery recipe for pruned LLMs on **free-form generation**. ShortOPD reports 1.6–4.4x the score of SFT, KD and SeqKD under matched budgets on 25%-pruned Qwen3-4B. The main failure mode it finds is **length-specific**: a freshly pruned model, rolled out to long horizons, spends its budget on repetitive suffixes, so OPD works best with a short-to-long rollout schedule. Counter-evidence also exists. NVIDIA's Semi-OPD (2610.11291) and a NeurIPS 2026 systematic study find that fresh on-policy rollouts are often unnecessary: one-time offline student rollouts match or beat OPD unless teacher and student are already highly aligned. All results are on reasoning, math and code LLMs. **No OCR, ASR or VLM-transcription OPD result was found.**

### Cited Findings

**Pruning-specific OPD papers (2026)**
- **ShortOPD** (arXiv 2607.13124, Jul 2026, marked "Work in Progress"). The pre-compression model is a frozen teacher, and the student trains on its own rollouts with dense token-level teacher supervision. The paper uses a short-to-long rollout schedule: it detects repetitive suffixes the teacher confirms, treats the surviving prefix as each rollout's effective length, and allocates future rollout budget to lengths the damaged model can currently use. NOT VERIFIED (snippet only). — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13124); [HF page](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.13124)
  - On 25%-pruned Qwen3-4B-Instruct: **48.46 average on generation tasks vs 21.19 for 1 epoch of SFT without KD**. Recovers "about two-thirds" of the unpruned teacher's generation score across 8 task families. — [arXiv html](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.13124v1)
  - About **9x** the unrecovered compressed model's score and **1.6–4.4x** standard recovery recipes (SFT w/o KD, KD, SeqKD), and beats sparse-reward RLVR under matched budgets. — [arXiv html](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.13124v1)
  - Table 3 compares against SeqKD and KD with the same frozen teacher. The extracted text suggests that moving the signal off-policy costs **~9 average points (SeqKD)**, and teacher-forced dense KD still trails. Snippet text was garbled, so verify the exact figure. — [arXiv pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.13124)
  - Ablation: the gains need **both** on-policy states **and** dense teacher distributions. Removing math or code prompts from the recovery corpus sharply hurts recovery on those capabilities. — [ShortOPD summary](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.13124)
  - **Length/compute:** matches a fixed 8,192-token rollout horizon to within 2 points using a 2,048 ceiling, with up to **71% fewer rollout tokens** and **8.5 h vs 35.9 h** training (about a quarter of the time). Rollout temperature 0.7. — [arXiv html](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.13124v1)
  - **Diagnosis relevant to long outputs:** after compression, greedy pass@1 on free-form generation "nearly vanishes" while pass@k recovers under repeated sampling, so the authors argue useful outputs are suppressed rather than lost. A long fixed budget reaches repetitive suffixes before the policy recovers, and a permanently short budget truncates legitimate long generations later. Single model family only (Qwen3). — [arXiv pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.13124)
- **LOOP: Recovery-Aligned Iterative Layer Pruning via On-Policy Distillation** (arXiv 2610.25679, Oct 2026, Liang Xu et al.). It alternates single-layer pruning with short OPD phases and reuses on-policy trajectories and recovery signals to score each remaining layer's reasoning contribution and recovery sensitivity. Reported **+11.46 points over one-shot pruning + OPD**. Ablation: the gain over one-shot "cannot be attributed to on-policy distillation alone", so iterative, recovery-aware selection matters. NOT VERIFIED (snippet only). — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25679); [arXiv html](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.25679)
- **ReCal: Calibrating Structured Pruning for On-Policy Distillation Recovery** (arXiv 2610.11332, Oct 2026). It changes the pruning calibration objective to forward KL between teacher and a pruned probe, to protect the predictions pruning disrupts, and then recovers with OPD. Reported gains of **up to 16.7 points on AIME** after OPD across models and pruning methods. NOT VERIFIED (snippet only). — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11332)
- **The Fragility and Dynamics of LLM Pruning-Recovery Steps** (arXiv 2610.15574). Uses GOLD (an on-policy KD trainer) for recovery. The snippet says the tested pruning-first strategy helps "only modestly, winning 31 of 45 matched pairs". The exact comparison was not visible, and a second search could not find more detail. NOT VERIFIED (snippet only, single aggregator line). — [arXiv html](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.15574)
- **Train Where the Quantized Model Goes** (arXiv 2609.26708). Adds an OPD stage for a quantized Qwen3-0.6B (~2.79 effective bits). Looping/repetition and budget exhaustion drop after OPD, which is the same degeneration failure mode as in pruning. NOT VERIFIED (snippet only). — [alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.26708)
- **Looped Latent Attention** (arXiv 2607.15456). KV compression; OPD raises MATH-500 by 0.16–0.24 (absolute, as reported) at 3.2–5.33x compression and reduces no-answer generations at every ratio. NOT VERIFIED (snippet only). — [arXiv pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.15456)
- **Closest VLM evidence: Structural Pruning of Large Vision Language Models** (arXiv 2604.24380). At high pruning ratios, **KD alone fails to beat SFT**, and SFT + KD gives the most robust recovery. This matches the user's CE+KD mix. It is not an OPD study. NOT VERIFIED (snippet only). — [arXiv pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.24380)
- **Minitron / Nemotron (NVIDIA)** recover pruned models with **off-policy logit distillation**, not OPD. Llama-3.1-Minitron-4B uses logit-only forward KL and drops the LM CE term. The NVIDIA blog guidance: use distillation loss alone; logit-only when depth changes little; add intermediate-state and embedding losses when depth is cut sharply. I found no on-policy stage in Minitron. NOT VERIFIED (snippet only). — [arXiv 2408.11796](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.11796); [NVIDIA blog](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/how-to-prune-and-distill-llama-3-1-8b-to-an-nvidia-llama-3-1-minitron-4b-model); [arXiv 2407.14679](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.14679v1)

**General OPD vs off-policy at (roughly) equal compute**
- **Qwen3 Technical Report** (arXiv 2505.09388, 2025). On Qwen3-8B, starting from the same off-policy-distilled checkpoint, **on-policy distillation reached 74.4% AIME'24 with ~1,800 GPU hours vs RL 67.6% with 17,920 GPU hours** (~1/10 compute; reported in Table 21 per secondary sources). Small Qwen3 models were built by strong-to-weak distillation (off-policy, then on-policy). NOT VERIFIED (snippet/secondary). — [Qwen3 report](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.09388); [Maginative](https://www.maginative.com/article/thinking-machines-claims-30x-cost-cut-for-training-ai-models/)
- **Thinking Machines blog "On-Policy Distillation"** (lead only, 2025). Qwen3-8B-Base student at 60% AIME'24 after 400K SFT examples reaches **70% after ~150 OPD steps** (Qwen3-32B teacher). Estimated **9–30x less compute** than extrapolated continued SFT. A review notes the SFT comparison is an extrapolation and the FLOPs accounting may understate teacher cost. "50–100x" figures quoted elsewhere come from a different self-distillation experiment. NOT VERIFIED (secondary only; blog not reachable). — [Maginative](https://www.maginative.com/thinking-machines-claims-30x-cost-cut-for-training-ai-models); [HF knowledge-base review](https://huggingface.co/datasets/rl-llm-wiki/knowledge-base/discussions/450)
- **Counter-evidence: "When Do We Need On-Policy Distillation? Distilling on Offline Student Rollouts Is Often Better"** (arXiv 2610.11291, NVIDIA, Oct 2026). **Semi-OPD** generates student rollouts once from the initial student, caches teacher token log-probs once, and reuses them; student probabilities are still recomputed each step. It often beats OPD in accuracy and efficiency. OPD helps only when teacher and student are highly aligned, measured by an output-token overlap ratio. For misaligned pairs, student rollouts become "increasingly off-policy w.r.t. the teacher **as context length grows**", which weakens the signal. NOT VERIFIED (snippet only). — [NVIDIA Research](https://research.nvidia.com/publication/2026-12_when-do-we-need-policy-distillation-distilling-offline-student-rollouts-often); [OpenTrain summary](https://www.opentrain.ai/papers/when-do-we-need-on-policy-distillation-distilling-on-offline-student-rollouts-is--arxiv-2610.11291/)
- **"On-Policy or Off-Policy Learning? A Systematic Study of Distillation Dynamics"** (NeurIPS 2026). Finds no consistent OnPD advantage over OffPD in final performance, forgetting, update sparsity or generalisation in its controlled setting. NOT VERIFIED (snippet only). — [NeurIPS 2026](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/172356)
- **"Are Full Rollouts Necessary for On-Policy Distillation?"** (May 2026). Truncated rollouts can be distilled on reliably, which cuts rollout cost. **Adaptive FastOPD** (arXiv 2607.29494) expands the rollout horizon based on progress. Both point the same way as ShortOPD's short-to-long schedule. NOT VERIFIED (snippet only). — [ModelWire](https://themodelwire.com/article/are-full-rollouts-necessary-for-on-policy-distillation-01KT0J7HKJ0XH783JWB624EGE3); [arXiv 2607.29494](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.29494)
- **Speculative KD (SKD)** (arXiv 2410.11325, ICLR 2025, Google). The student proposes tokens and the teacher replaces those outside its top-K, which interpolates between supervised KD and OPD. Gemma-7B→Gemma-2B: vs SFT, +41.8% COMET on Assamese→English translation, +230% ROUGE-L on dialogue summarization, +160% GSM8K. It consistently beats both supervised KD and on-policy KD for K in 5–50, and also beats them on 1k-prompt low-data runs. NOT VERIFIED (snippet only). — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.11325); [Google Research](https://research.google/pubs/speculative-knowledge-distillation-bridging-the-teacher-student-gap-through-interleaved-sampling/)
- **DistiLLM-2** (arXiv 2503.07067, ICML 2025). Contrastive objective: skew-KL on teacher-generated outputs and skew-reverse-KL on student-generated outputs. Reported best pass@1 on HumanEval/MBPP over GKD and DistiLLM. Pure teacher generations and pure student generations work best for their respective terms. Student samples are batch-collected per epoch, so not strictly on-policy. No numbers retrieved. NOT VERIFIED (snippet only). — [alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2503.07067); [ICML 2025](https://icml.cc/virtual/2025/poster/43884)
- **Survey of OPD for LLMs** (arXiv 2604.00626) frames OPD as fixing exposure bias: off-policy students get no gradient on how to behave in self-induced off-distribution states. NOT VERIFIED (snippet only). — [arXiv html](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.00626v1)

**Does OPD help long outputs more?**
- **ImitKD** (Lin et al., EMNLP 2020; arXiv 2009.07253, background). On MT, BLEU gains are largest in the longest-sequence bins: "the improvement is most significant for longer sequences". This is direct evidence that student-state training helps more as length grows. NOT VERIFIED (snippet only). — [arXiv html](https://arxiv.org/html/2009.07253)
- **arXiv 2409.12512** ("Exploring and Enhancing the Transfer of Distribution in KD for AR LMs"). ImitKD lowers the exposure-bias metric **ExAccErr** (excess accumulated error over generation steps), but its ROUGE-L is weak. **Student-generated outputs from a poor student can impair distillation**, and the teacher-student size gap matters. NOT VERIFIED (snippet only). — [arXiv html](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.12512)
- ShortOPD (above): the length effect cuts both ways for a freshly pruned model. Long on-policy rollouts degenerate into teacher-confirmed repetition, so short-to-long is needed. — [arXiv html](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.13124v1)
- Semi-OPD (above): divergence between student rollouts and teacher grows with context length for misaligned pairs. — [NVIDIA Research](https://research.nvidia.com/publication/2026-12_when-do-we-need-policy-distillation-distilling-offline-student-rollouts-often)

### Inferences
- For the 0.8B → 0.3B/0.1B OCR student, the strongest transferable signal is ShortOPD's diagnosis. Heavily pruned models fail on long free-form generation through **repetition loops and collapse of greedy decoding**, not through lost knowledge. Teacher-forced KD (the current recipe) never shows the student its own loops. In OCR at ~1,500 tokens/page, the typical symptoms would be repeated lines, skipped or duplicated table rows, and runaway output. If the pruned checkpoints show these at greedy decoding, a short OPD stage is the most targeted fix (inference).
- Recommended low-risk ordering (inference from ShortOPD, Semi-OPD and SKD): (1) keep the current off-policy CE+KD stage; (2) add a short final OPD stage with the **0.8B unpruned model** (or the 4B teacher) as teacher, scoring student rollouts token-wise with reverse KL; (3) cap rollout length short at first (e.g. a few hundred tokens or per-region crops) and grow toward full pages; (4) as a cheaper alternative, use Semi-OPD-style **one-time offline student rollouts** with cached teacher logprobs. This fits the user's existing stored-logprob pipeline.
- Teacher choice matters for OPD. The Semi-OPD overlap-ratio criterion suggests the **unpruned 0.8B parent** (shares tokenizer and architecture, highest token overlap with the pruned child) may be a better on-policy teacher than the 4B, especially for the 0.1B model. This would be a cheap measurement to run before committing (inference).
- OCR transcription is far more "anchored" by the image than open-ended reasoning, so the exposure-bias gap may be smaller than on AIME/code. The OPD gain magnitudes above (2–4x) should not be expected to transfer. They come from settings where the pruned model's greedy pass@1 had nearly collapsed (inference).
- Compute estimate (inference): OPD needs student generation (~1,500 tokens/page at 0.1–0.3B, cheap) plus one teacher forward per rollout. ShortOPD's 71% rollout-token saving from short-to-long suggests most of the cost can be avoided early.

## D7: Sequence-level vs token-level KD for long, near-deterministic outputs; does soft-label KL add value over CE on teacher labels when the teacher is near-deterministic?

### Takeaway
In MT, sequence-level KD (training on teacher beam/greedy outputs) has long beaten word-level KD under greedy decoding: +4.2 vs +0.7 BLEU in Kim & Rush (2016). Combining both gives further gains, and this is essentially the user's CE-on-teacher-labels + forward-KL mix. The closest analogue to OCR is ASR: Distil-Whisper uses exactly **teacher pseudo-labels (sequence-level) + token-level KL + WER-based filtering of pseudo-labels**. It reports students within 1% WER of the teacher out of distribution, with fewer long-form hallucinations. Evidence that soft labels add little when the teacher is near-deterministic is **indirect**. Seq-KD's known benefit is mostly "simplifying" the target distribution. Ranking is task-dependent (word-KD won in speech translation), and f-DISTILL finds soft-label sequence-level variants beat hard SeqKD. **No paper was found that measures dark-knowledge value as a function of teacher entropy for low-entropy generation tasks.**

### Cited Findings

**Classic MT evidence (background)**
- **Kim & Rush 2016, Sequence-Level KD** (arXiv 1606.07947, EMNLP 2016). On En→De with a 2x500 student, **Seq-KD +4.2 BLEU vs Word-KD +0.7 BLEU with greedy decoding**. Seq-KD + Word-KD combined: **+4.0 (2x500) / +3.8 (2x300)** further BLEU with greedy decoding (per review). Overall 4.2/1.7 BLEU better than no-distillation baseline with greedy/beam. NOT VERIFIED (snippet/review only). — [Liner review](https://liner.com/review/sequencelevel-knowledge-distillation); [arXiv pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.07947)
- Compression survey (arXiv 2008.05221): Seq-KD beats Word-KD on En→De but performs similarly on Th→En, and combining yields further gains. NOT VERIFIED (snippet only). — [arXiv pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.05221)
- **Counter-example: direct speech translation** (arXiv 2012.04964). **Word-KD achieves the best results by a large margin**; the authors conclude the best seq2seq KD technique is task-dependent. NOT VERIFIED (snippet only). — [arXiv pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.04964)
- "Towards Understanding and Improving KD for NMT" (arXiv 2305.08096): the context difference may explain why sequence-level KD generally outperforms word-level KD. NOT VERIFIED (snippet only). — [arXiv html](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.08096)
- **KD4MT survey** (arXiv 2602.15845, 2026): terminology only was retrieved. Seq-KD = "hard distillation" (one-hot labels, offline); Word-KD = "soft distillation" (full vocab distribution). Its comparative conclusions were not retrieved. NOT VERIFIED (snippet only). — [arXiv pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.15845)

**LLM-era sequence vs token level**
- **f-DISTILL** (Wen, Li, Du, Mou; arXiv 2307.15190, ACL 2023). Sequence-level KD as generalized f-divergence, decomposed step-wise into tractable word-level losses (an upper bound for TVD). SeqKD and ENGINE are shown to be approximations. Its four variants (KL, RKL, JS, TVD) **consistently outperform SeqKD and ENGINE** across 4 tasks. **Symmetric divergences (JS, TVD) beat asymmetric ones** by avoiding mode-averaging and mode-collapse. The soft-label approach "provides more information than hard SeqKD". Cost: f-DISTILL needs teacher soft probabilities, so it is less efficient than SeqKD. Exact tables not retrieved. NOT VERIFIED (snippet only). — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15190); [ACL pdf](https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-long.605.pdf)
- **Revisiting KD for Autoregressive LMs / ATKD** (Zhong et al., arXiv 2402.11890, ACL 2024). Larger teachers can give dramatically poorer students. Different tokens have different "teaching modes" (roughly: easy/confident tokens vs hard ones), and uniform treatment degrades KD. ATKD gives up to **+3.04% average** over baseline KD methods on OPT, Pythia and LLaMA. A SeqKD vs word-KD table was not retrieved. NOT VERIFIED (snippet only). — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.587/); [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.11890)

**ASR analogue (closest to OCR): Distil-Whisper** (Gandhi et al., arXiv 2311.00430, 2023)
- Objective = weighted sum of **pseudo-label CE (teacher transcripts; "sequence-level distillation signal" and consistent formatting)** + **token-level KL** to the full teacher distribution. A follow-up reports weights α_KL = 0.8, α_PL = 1.0 as in Gandhi et al. NOT VERIFIED (snippet only). — [arXiv pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.00430); [uDistil-Whisper, NAACL 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.naacl-long.296.pdf)
- **WER filter:** pseudo-labelled examples are dropped when normalised WER vs ground truth exceeds λ. Table 10 shows validation WER improving as λ is lowered (exact values not retrieved). NOT VERIFIED (snippet only). — [arXiv pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.00430)
- Objective ablation includes PL, KL, MSE. One visible row: **PL + KL = 12.6 ID / 7.4 OOD / 11.4 avg WER**. Other rows' values were not retrievable, so it is not confirmed whether PL+KL beats PL alone. NOT VERIFIED (snippet only). — [arXiv pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.00430)
- Headline: about 49% smaller and 5.8x faster, **within 1% WER** of the teacher on OOD short-form, and better than the teacher on long-form thanks to **fewer hallucinations/repetitions**. NOT VERIFIED (snippet only). — [arXiv pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.00430); [GitHub](https://github.com/huggingface/distil-whisper)
- **uDistil-Whisper** (arXiv 2407.01257, NAACL 2025) does label-free filtering of pseudo-labels (no ground truth needed) for low-data regimes, reusing the PL+KL objective. NOT VERIFIED (snippet only). — [arXiv pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.01257)

**Error compounding with length / exposure bias**
- ImitKD: gains concentrate on the longest sequences (see D3). — [arXiv html](https://arxiv.org/html/2009.07253)
- Exposure-bias metric ExAccErr (excess accumulated error vs generation step) used in 2409.12512. ImitKD reduces it, but student-generated data from a weak student hurts. — [arXiv html](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.12512)
- ShortOPD: repetition/degeneration in long rollouts of pruned models (see D3). — [arXiv html](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.13124v1)

**Low-entropy teachers / dark knowledge**
- "Consistently Informative Soft-Label Temperature for KD" (arXiv 2605.20357). Under a fixed temperature, some samples stay **overconfident/low-entropy and uninformative** while others are oversmoothed. This argues for per-sample temperature. Classification setting only. NOT VERIFIED (snippet only). — [arXiv pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.20357)

### Inferences
- **The user's recipe is already the Distil-Whisper / Kim & Rush "combined" recipe** (CE on teacher pseudo-labels + token-level KL). Both literatures favour the combination over either alone. The strongest cheap addition from the ASR analogue is **filtering teacher pseudo-labels by quality** before CE. Examples: CER vs ground truth where it exists, or label-free proxies such as teacher mean logprob, repetition detection, or agreement between two teachers. This is a direct transfer of Distil-Whisper's WER-λ filter (inference).
- **When the teacher is near-deterministic, what can token-level KL still add over CE on teacher labels?** In theory, at tokens where the teacher puts ~1.0 on its argmax, forward KL ≈ CE on that token, so KL adds almost nothing there. The value of soft labels concentrates on the **minority of high-entropy tokens**: ambiguous glyphs, formatting/markup choices, reading-order decisions, table delimiters, math LaTeX variants. This is consistent with ATKD's finding that tokens differ in teaching mode, and with the low-entropy-uninformative observation in 2605.20357. It also implies top-20 stored logprobs are more than enough for most tokens (inference, not measured in any OCR paper found).
- Practical implication (inference): token-weighted KD (upweight KL on high-teacher-entropy tokens, or apply KL only where teacher entropy > threshold), or a symmetric divergence (JS/TVD per f-DISTILL) on those tokens, is the most targeted change. Uniform 0.5/0.5 CE/KL mixing mostly duplicates CE on the ~90%+ near-deterministic tokens.
- Sequence-level KD's documented benefit in MT is partly **target simplification**: teacher outputs are more consistent than human references. For OCR this maps to consistent formatting conventions (markdown, tables, LaTeX). That argues for producing all CE targets from the same teacher rather than mixing human and teacher labels inconsistently (inference; matches Distil-Whisper's "consistent transcription formatting" rationale).
- Neither seq-KD nor token-KD fixes exposure bias, since both are teacher-forced. If long-page degeneration appears in the pruned students, D3's OPD/Semi-OPD stage is the complement, not a switch between seq and token KD (inference).
